Who we are and why we are here

THERAPY is a neurowellness centre in Eltham, created to bring mental and physical wellbeing together. We bring movement, recovery, mental health support and food into one welcoming place. Our name was chosen deliberately. Therapy comes in many forms, and we want caring for yourself to feel approachable, without stigma or the pressure to do wellness perfectly.

Food belongs in that story. Your gut and brain are connected, and the way you eat is one part of caring for your whole self. Our café brings that philosophy to the table through flavour, variety and ingredients with a purpose. It is also a place to pause, share a meal and feel connected. You can begin with a coffee, a conversation or simply a little time for yourself.

We don’t do perfect wellness. We do real life.

I

Breakfast

To begin the day, unhurried
Avocado Toast

Smashed avocado on sourdough with lemon, cracked pepper, chopped pickles, Persian feta and pomegranate pearls. Finished with toasted pepitas and silky beetroot hummus.

Colour and crunch with a little brain science. Pepita protein contains tryptophan, an amino acid used to make serotonin. Avocado and vegetables add fibre for the gut side of your gut–mind connection.

Eggs Your Way

Free-range eggs, fried, scrambled or poached, on sourdough with extra virgin olive oil and sea salt flakes.

Eggs bring choline for acetylcholine, a messenger involved in attention and memory. Their protein also contains tyrosine, which the body uses to make dopamine. Everyday ingredients with a role in normal brain chemistry.

Sweet Potato and Zucchini Fritters

Sweet potato, zucchini and almond meal fritters layered with avocado, topped with a kale micro salad and pesto.

More plants, more variety for your gut. Vegetables and almonds bring different fibres to your plate; some fibres feed the microbes that participate in the ongoing conversation between gut and brain.

Eggs Benedict

Poached eggs and smoked salmon on golden crumpets with crème fraîche and hollandaise. Finished with beetroot hummus and wok-tossed greens.

Salmon contributes DHA, a fat found in brain-cell membranes. Salmon and egg proteins also provide tryptophan, the starting ingredient for serotonin. A breakfast to savour, with nourishment behind the flavour.

Chia and Coconut Bowl

Chia pudding made with coconut milk, topped with coconut yoghurt, fresh kiwi, mixed berries and toasted coconut flakes.

A fresh way to nourish your gut. Chia, kiwi and berries contribute fibre for regular digestion. Your gut is part of a two-way communication system with your brain, making digestive care part of whole-person wellbeing.

Coconut Porridge

Oats cooked in coconut milk, topped with fresh berries, a drizzle of pure maple syrup and a dusting of cinnamon.

A warm bowl for an unhurried start. Oats provide carbohydrate for energy, including fuel for the brain, and beta-glucan fibre that some gut microbes can ferment. Everyday nourishment, one spoonful at a time.

Add-ons — feta, avocado, mushrooms, roasted tomato or baby spinach · egg, smoked salmon or bacon · crunchy chilli oil, hollandaise or pesto · sourdough or gluten-free toast
Make it yours. Extra vegetables bring more plant variety; an egg or smoked salmon adds protein.
II

Lunch & Late Afternoon

Mains
French Lentil Bowl

House-cooked French lentils with lemon and olive oil dressing, fresh herbs, red cabbage, wild rocket, golden sultanas and toasted sunflower seeds. Topped with smoky babaganoush and hemp crisps. Enjoy on its own or add a poached egg, chicken, lamb or catch of the day.

Lentils bring plant protein containing tyrosine, an amino acid used to make dopamine. Their fermentable fibre also feeds gut microbes. Plant variety connects the story, from digestive nourishment to the building blocks of brain chemistry.

Golden Seared Barramundi

Pan-seared, steam-baked barramundi with fresh basil pesto, quinoa, pico de gallo, sliced avocado and micro coriander.

Barramundi protein contains tryptophan, which the body uses to make serotonin, a messenger involved in mood, sleep and appetite. Quinoa, avocado and vegetables add plant variety and fibre alongside the fish.

Thai Chicken Salad

Sous-vide lemongrass chicken finished on the chargrill, with buckwheat noodles, mango, red onion and cucumber. Tossed with lime and green chilli dressing, mint, coriander and Thai basil; finished with roasted cashews and chilli flakes.

Chicken contributes vitamin B6, which helps enzymes make neurotransmitters including serotonin, dopamine and GABA. GABA helps regulate nerve-cell activity. Fresh herbs, vegetables and cashews bring variety to the gut side of the story.

III

Sides

For the middle of the table
Fish Tacos

Corn tortillas filled with barramundi, red cabbage, creamy coriander dressing, diced avocado and salsa. Vegan option with black beans.

Food to enjoy together, with plants in every bite. Cabbage and avocado contribute fibre; the black-bean option adds fermentable fibres that feed gut microbes. Sharing the table makes room for conversation, too.

Sweet Potato Wedges

House-made sweet potato wedges with spiced chilli salt, coconut yoghurt and chilli oil.

Familiar comfort with everyday nourishment. Sweet potato supplies carbohydrate, which the body can use to fuel the brain, alongside fibre for digestion. A warm side to savour at your own pace.

Kingfish Crudo

Sliced raw kingfish with kaffir lime, coriander, green chilli dressing and touches of coconut cream.

Delicate fish with a role in everyday brain nourishment. Kingfish protein supplies tyrosine, used to make dopamine, a messenger involved in motivation, learning and movement. Bright citrus and fresh herbs invite you to savour each bite.

Nachos

House-baked corn chips with melted cashew cheese, black beans, pico de gallo and avocado. Enjoy on their own or add a poached egg, chicken, lamb or catch of the day.

Made for the middle of the table. Black beans bring plant protein and fermentable fibre for gut microbes. Add conversation and a shared meal: caring for yourself can include both nourishment and enjoyment.

Burrata

Activated charcoal-coated burrata with balsamic-glazed strawberries, fresh basil and focaccia. Gluten-free option available.

Creamy burrata brings dairy protein containing tryptophan, an amino acid the body uses to make serotonin. Paired with strawberries and basil, it is also an invitation to slow down, share and enjoy your food.

Wok Tossed Greens

Wok-seared bok choy and kale with wilted rocket and baby spinach, finished with black sesame seeds.

A small side with a place in brain nutrition. Leafy greens contain folate, a B vitamin involved in normal nervous-system function. Adding different vegetables also builds variety across your everyday meals.

Fresh Greens

Bok choy, kale, wild rocket and micro herbs, lightly dressed with lemon and extra virgin olive oil.

A fresh addition to your gut–mind routine. Different leaves bring plant variety and fibre to your meal. Nourishing the gut is an everyday practice, built through the foods you enjoy over time.
